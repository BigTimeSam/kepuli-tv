Privacy Policy

Applies to the Kepuli-TV browser extension for Chrome and Firefox and to the Kepuli-TV app for Android phones, tablets and televisions. Effective 13 November 2026, version 1.7

In short

  • No data is sent to the developer — except the answer you may choose to give in the one-question form that opens when you uninstall the extension. No analytics, no tracking, no ads, no developer account.
  • Settings and personal lists are stored on your own device — and, only if you turn on sync, in your browser's own sync storage, which your browser carries to your other computers. Credentials are sent to the service you configure to authenticate requests.
  • The developer never receives, sees or stores any of your data. The extension has no backend.
  • Network requests go to the addresses you point it at — your own IPTV server or playlist, and the programme guide it names — and, for channel logos, to GitHub's file server, which hosts a public collection of logos (see section 3).
  • The extension cannot see the pages you browse. It has no content scripts and requests access to no website at install time.
  • The Android app is the same player. It is the extension, run by a browser engine of its own inside the app, so everything here holds for it too. What differs on Android (what Android is asked to allow, what the engine itself looks up, and what Google Play tells the developer) is in section 6.

1.What Kepuli-TV is

Kepuli-TV is a browser extension that plays back the content of the user's own Xtream Codes IPTV account, M3U playlist or Stalker portal in a browser tab. It ships no media, no channel list and no accounts of its own: it is a player, and plays nothing until the user adds an account or a playlist from their own provider.

The developer and author of this policy is Samuli Vainio, a private individual. Kepuli-TV is a personal project, not a company product. Contact: samuli.vainio@gmail.com.

2.What data the extension handles

Everything below is created by the user's own actions inside the extension's own tab and is stored locally only, in the browser's per-extension storage (chrome.storage.local and IndexedDB) on the device where it is used.

DataWhy it is keptWhere it lives
Connection settings of each account you add: server address, port, username and password, a playlist address and a programme guide address, or a Stalker portal address and the MAC address your provider registered, and the name you give the account To connect to your own IPTV server without retyping the credentials on every launch chrome.storage.local
Player preferences: subtitle language and size, stream mode, adult-category hiding, external player template To remember interface and playback settings chrome.storage.local
Favourites, watch history, resume positions, and the titles of the programmes you have watched for a few minutes (for the picks on the empty player's page) To show your own lists and continue playback where it stopped chrome.storage.local
Your choice whether to sync, and when it last synced To sync only when you asked for it chrome.storage.local
Recordings you make, and the recordings you have planned To play them back or save them as files when you choose The browser's private file storage for the extension (origin private file system) and chrome.storage.local; never synced
Catalogue cache: channel, movie and series listings, categories and programme guide data To avoid re-downloading the whole catalogue on every launch IndexedDB

Sync between computers is off unless you turn it on (Settings → Account & data). When it is on, your accounts, settings, favourites, channel lists, reminders, and the newest of your watch history and resume positions are also written to the browser's sync storage (chrome.storage.sync), which Chrome keeps in your Google account and Firefox in your Firefox account, and which they bring to the computers signed in to the same account. That storage is the browser's, governed by Google's or Mozilla's terms; the developer has no access to it. Passwords go there only if you also tick Include passwords; without it, an Xtream account goes without its password, a programme guide address that carries credentials goes blank, the stream addresses of your favourites and history stay behind (they often carry your user name and password), and a playlist whose address carries credentials does not go at all, nor do its lists. Without it, a Stalker portal account does not go either, as its MAC address works as a password. The lists are named in the sync storage by a code made from the account, not by the account's address. Turning sync off stops it; the catalogue cache is never synced.

The extension does not ask for or handle names, email addresses, location, payment information, health information, contacts, messages or browsing history.

3.Where data is sent

The extension has no server or cloud service of its own and sends nothing to the developer. Network traffic occurs only as follows:

  • Your own IPTV server. The extension fetches catalogues, programme data and media streams from the address you enter in the settings. As the Xtream Codes API works, the username and password travel in the request URL. Your provider sees this traffic, including your IP address and what you watch — governed by their terms and privacy policy, not by this one.
  • Your playlist and its programme guide. For an M3U playlist account, the extension downloads the playlist from the address you enter, and the XMLTV programme guide from the address you enter or the one the playlist names. Those servers see the request, including your IP address.
  • Your Stalker portal. For a Stalker portal account, the extension talks to the portal address you enter as a MAG set-top box does: the MAC address you enter is sent in a cookie, and the request names a MAG box in its User-Agent. A recording is made from the same streams you could watch, and stays on your device.
  • Your browser's sync, only if you turn it on. See section 2. The browser, not the extension, sends that data to Google's or Mozilla's servers.
  • When you uninstall the extension. The browser opens a one-question Google Forms page asking why. Its address carries the extension's version, the browser (Chrome or Firefox) and the interface language, pre-filled into the form, and nothing else: no account, server, playlist or viewing information. Nothing is sent unless you choose an answer and press Submit; the form does not collect your email address or ask you to sign in. Answers go to the developer through Google Forms, under Google's privacy policy. You can simply close the page.
  • Channel logos. Many providers' logo addresses lead nowhere, so for each channel the extension first looks for its logo in tv-logos, a public collection of TV channel logos on GitHub, and loads a match from raw.githubusercontent.com. The list of the collection's files ships inside the extension, so the lookup itself sends nothing; only the image is loaded, as any web page loads an image. GitHub sees your IP address, your browser details and which logo files are loaded, and so could tell which channels are in the lists you open. The request carries no account, server, credentials or identifier of yours. Where the collection has no match, the logo comes from the address your provider gave.
  • Cover art and your provider's logos. The browser loads these images from the addresses your provider put in the listing (an address to a file page on github.com is read as the same file on raw.githubusercontent.com, which is where GitHub serves it). Those may point to a third-party server, which then sees your IP address and browser details, exactly as when any web page loads an image. The extension does not choose these addresses and adds no identifiers to them.
  • Handoff to an external player. If you use the handoff feature (VLC, IINA, mpv), the extension builds a playlist file or a URL and gives it to your operating system. The address contains your IPTV credentials, but it does not leave your device: it is passed locally to the player you installed, which opens the stream from your own server.
  • Links you click. A film's or a series' IMDb page and trailer on YouTube, a search on IMDb for a person in its credits, and Help, which opens the user guide on GitHub, open in a new tab only when you click them; that site then sees the visit as it sees any other. The IMDb search sends the person's name, and nothing about your account or what you watch.
  • Copy to clipboard and playlist files. A stream URL is copied only when you explicitly click to copy it, and a playlist file (.m3u) is saved only when you choose to. Both contain your credentials: treat them like your password, and take care where you paste or share them.

4.Credential storage and security

Your IPTV credentials are stored unencrypted in the browser's extension storage, as local extension data generally is. Anyone with access to your user account or browser profile on that device can read them. Do not use the extension on a shared or unattended computer if that is a concern.

Credentials are sent as-is to the address you supply. If your server uses http, that traffic is unencrypted on the network too — use an https address whenever your provider offers one.

5.Permissions and why they are requested

  • storage — to keep settings, credentials, favourites, history and resume positions on the device, and, if you turn sync on, in the browser's sync storage.
  • declarativeNetRequestWithHostAccess — for a Stalker portal account only: a rule for the duration of the browser session puts the MAC address cookie and a MAG box's User-Agent on the requests to that portal's address alone, which a page cannot do itself. It acts only on addresses you have granted access to, changes no other request and is gone when the browser closes.
  • alarms — to keep the time of a reminder or a planned recording while the player is closed, so that the recording starts and the reminder is said. Nothing leaves the device.
  • notifications (optional) — not requested at install time. The browser asks when you set your first reminder, or when you turn the notifications on in the settings; a reminder is then shown as a notification while the player is closed or out of view. It names the programme and its channel and is shown on your device only.
  • unlimitedStorage — for the catalogue cache and recordings. A typical IPTV catalogue is tens of megabytes and exceeds the browser's default quota.
  • Optional host access (http://*/*, https://*/*) — not requested at install time. The browser asks for permission only when you enter your own server address, and grants it for that address alone. It is needed because your server's address cannot be known in advance. If your provider redirects its streams to another server, the browser does not reveal which one; the player then offers Allow all addresses, and access to every address is granted only if you click it and accept the browser's prompt. It is used solely to play your provider's streams.

The extension has no content scripts: it does not read, modify or observe any other tab or web page. It operates solely within its own tab.

6.The Android app

The app for Android phones, tablets and televisions (Google Play, io.github.bigtimesam.kepulitv) is the same player: the extension's own files, run inside the app by GeckoView, the engine of Firefox for Android, which is part of the app. Sections 1 to 5 describe it as they describe the extension, with these differences.

  • Where the data lives. Everything in section 2 is kept in the app's own private storage on the device, which other apps cannot read. There is no browser account behind the app, so nothing is synced to another device, whatever the sync setting says.
  • No uninstall question. Removing the app opens no form and sends nothing.
  • Your server's address. There is no browser to ask on the app's behalf, so the app allows itself the addresses the player asks for (your server, and where your provider sends its streams) without a prompt. It still talks only to the addresses in section 3.
  • The engine keeps to itself. GeckoView is a browser's engine, and a browser's engine left alone asks a few things of Mozilla and Google when it starts (whether a sign-in page stands in the way of the network, which country the device is in, lists of harmful sites, the engine's own settings). The app turns all of those off: measured on a newly installed app (version 1.13.0.1, three minutes with no account added), it looked up no address at all. Android itself checks the network in its own way, as it does for every app.
  • What Google Play tells the developer. Google Play installs and updates the app and shows the developer totals: how many devices have it, of what kind, in which countries, and, if you have agreed to share diagnostics with Google, crash reports without your account or viewing data. That is collected by Google under Google's privacy policy, not by the app, and nothing in it names you, your provider or what you watch.
  • What Android is asked to allow. Network access, for your server. On a phone or a tablet also: notifications, asked when you turn reminders on; running in the foreground while something plays, with its notification, so that the sound goes on with the screen off or another app in front; keeping the device awake for the stream meanwhile; and starting after a restart of the device, only to set the reminders' alarms again. For a reminder to come while the app is closed, the programme's name, its channel and its time are kept in the app's storage until the programme is over. None of this leaves the device.
  • Files. A file you save from the player (a playlist for another player, your settings, a recording) goes to the device's Downloads, where other apps you allow can read it; a playlist file contains your credentials. A file you pick for the player is copied into the app's own storage for reading and replaced by the next one.
  • Links and other players. A link opens in the device's browser, and a handoff to an external player in that player's app (section 3), not inside Kepuli-TV.

7.Remote code

The extension neither loads nor executes any code from outside its package. All executable code — including the media libraries and the WebAssembly audio decoder — ships inside the extension package. Only media and catalogue data are fetched from the network; never code.

8.Deleting your data

  • Cache: can be cleared from the extension's settings at any time (Settings → Clear cache).
  • Everything the extension stored: Settings → Reset everything (“reset”) removes the credentials, preferences, favourites, watch history, resume positions and the whole cache database, and what the extension put in the browser's sync storage.
  • On Android: the same settings are in the app, and Android's own Settings → Apps → Kepuli-TV → Storage → Clear storage, or removing the app, deletes everything the app stored. Files you saved to Downloads are yours and stay until you delete them.
  • Everything: uninstalling the extension removes all data it stored on the device. There is nothing for the developer to delete, because nothing was ever sent to the developer — apart from an uninstall answer you may have chosen to give, which carries nothing that identifies you.

9.Sale and transfer of data

User data is not sold or transferred to third parties. It is not used or transferred for purposes unrelated to the extension's single purpose, nor to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes. This is guaranteed technically: the developer receives no data at all, apart from the anonymous answer to the uninstall question, if you choose to give one.

10.Children

The extension is not directed at children under 13 and collects no data from them — as it collects none from anyone.

11.Data controller and GDPR

Because the developer neither receives, processes nor stores personal data, using the extension creates no personal data register held by the developer. The data on your device is under your control and can be deleted as described above. Data subject requests (access, rectification, erasure, portability) are in practice directed at your IPTV provider, which is its own controller.
