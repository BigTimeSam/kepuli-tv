# MKV in the browser, with sound and subtitles

Browsers do not play MKV files, and most IPTV providers serve their films
and series as MKV. Kepuli-TV unpacks the file as it arrives into a form the
browser does play, so a film starts in the tab without a download and
without an external player.

## What plays

- **Seeking** anywhere in the file, and a resume point when you come back.
- **Every audio track**, chosen under the picture by language and format.
- **AC-3, E-AC-3 and DTS** sound, which browsers do not decode: the player
  decodes them itself. For the few tracks it cannot, **Play without sound**
  is on offer.
- **Text subtitles** from the file, drawn by the player.

## Subtitles your way

The **Aa** button beside the subtitle selector, and **Settings**, choose the
look: shadow, outline, yellow, box or high contrast, at a size of your
choosing. `+` and `−` change the size while you watch. The language you
chose carries over to the following episodes. Subtitles stored as pictures
rather than text are not shown.

## Before you click

A file the browser cannot play is marked in the list before you open it.
Under the picture a film shows its container, picture, sound and subtitles,
so you can see what the file holds.

The picture itself is decoded by the browser. Whether a given video format
plays therefore depends on your browser and device, and the mark in the
list tells which files will.

## When the browser cannot

The player's menu hands the stream to VLC or another player on your
computer. On a phone the item reads **Open in VLC**.

> [!WARNING]
> The address handed over holds your user name and password, so treat it
> as you treat the password.

## Where it works

In [Chrome](/iptv-player-chrome/) and [Firefox](/iptv-player-firefox/), in
[Firefox for Android](/iptv-player-android/), and on the
[iPhone](/iptv-player-iphone/) in Orion.
