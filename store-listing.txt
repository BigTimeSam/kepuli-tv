=========================================

--- SHORT DESCRIPTION (field "Description", max 132 characters) ------------
The same text is in manifest.json, from which the store reads it
automatically. Length 117 characters.

Xtream Codes IPTV player for the browser: channels, programme guide, movies and series — MKV with subtitles included.


--- LONG DESCRIPTION (field "Detailed description", max 16,000 characters) --

Kepuli-TV plays the contents of your own Xtream Codes IPTV account straight in
Chrome. No separate application and no media server: you give the server
address and your credentials, and the channels, movies and series are in the
list.

PLEASE NOTE: The extension contains, distributes and relays no content, no
channels and no providers. You need your own Xtream Codes IPTV account, which
you have obtained yourself. Kepuli-TV is a player and nothing else, and it is
intended solely for viewing content you have the right to view. You are
responsible for ensuring that the service you use is lawful in your own
country.


IT PLAYS WHAT THE BROWSER CANNOT

Chrome will not take Matroska, even when the picture inside it would be fine.
Kepuli-TV unpacks the MKV container on the fly into fMP4 and feeds it to the
browser's own player — the picture is neither decoded nor re-encoded, so the
work is light and seeking behaves normally.

The audio decides more often than the container: more than half of the
episodes use AC-3, E-AC-3 or DTS, none of which exist in Chrome. Those are
decoded with a wasm decoder and encoded back to AAC with the browser's own
encoder.

Measured on a sample of 23,628 episodes: without these, 44.6% would play; with
the unpacking, 93.7%.


SUBTITLES

The subtitle tracks of MKV files are picked up during the unpacking and handed
to the browser, so they show in full-screen mode too. Below the player there is
a language selector in alphabetical order and three sizes. The chosen language
is remembered and carries over to the next episode of a series.

The supported formats are SRT, ASS/SSA and WebVTT. Bitmap subtitles (PGS,
VOBSUB) are images and do not show in the browser.


FAST EVEN WITH ENORMOUS LISTS

A typical playlist is tens of megabytes and hundreds of thousands of lines.
Kepuli-TV does not load it in one go. It fetches from the player_api.php API
only what you are browsing at the time: the categories when the connection
opens, a country's channels only when you click the country, the whole list
only if you search it. A country's channels typically open within a second or
two.

Everything loaded once stays in the IndexedDB cache, so the next open is
instant. The cache can be cleared from the settings.


CATEGORIES IN A SENSIBLE SHAPE

Providers pack two levels into a single string: "Sweden - Sport", "Albania -
Movies Club". Kepuli-TV splits them, which condenses hundreds of muddled
categories into dozens of countries in the sidebar and moves the subjects into
topic buttons along the top.

Lists are always sorted alphabetically, numbers as numbers included: Rocky 2
comes before Rocky 10. The sidebar search matches sub-categories too: with the
word "sport" you find the countries that have sports channels even when the
country's name lacks it.


PROGRAMME GUIDE AND ARCHIVE

The guide (the g key) turns the whole window into a grid: channels as rows,
time on the horizontal axis and a moving now line. The video continues in the
corner. The timeline runs from two days back to five days forward, and on
channels that have an archive a past programme can be started straight from the
guide.


FEATURES

• Programme data on the list rows: the programme on air and a progress bar
• A full-window programme guide, and catch-up on channels with an archive
• MKV playback and subtitles without an external player
• Series by season, with cover art and episode descriptions
• Movie details: plot, running time, rating, codec
• Unplayable files are marked in the list before you click
• Hand-off to a desktop player (VLC and the like) with the resume position
• Resume positions remembered for movies and episodes
• The interface in English and Finnish
• Favourites and viewing history as tabs of their own
• A whole category as a favourite: its channels open straight from the
  favourites list, always fresh
• Automatic reconnection when a live stream drops
• Technical details over the picture: resolution, bitrate, playback engine
• Account details in view: validity and the number of concurrent connections
• Keyboard control: / search, arrow keys in the list, space pause,
  f full screen, m mute, n and p next and previous, g programme guide,
  x hand over to an external player


WHAT THE BROWSER PLAYS

Live channels are played as an MPEG-TS stream and, when needed, as HLS. In
automatic mode TS is tried first for its lower latency, and HLS takes over if
it does not start.

Of movies and episodes, .mp4, .m4v, .mov, .webm, .ts, .flv and .mkv play. Left
out are the AVI container, VC-1 and 10-bit H.264. Those are marked in the list
before you click, and their address goes to a desktop player or to the
clipboard with a single button.


PRIVACY AND PERMISSIONS

• All data stays on your own machine. The extension sends your details
  nowhere: network requests go only to your own server. The exception is
  channel logos and cover art, which the browser fetches from the addresses
  your provider put in the listing — those may point elsewhere.
• No tracking, no analytics, no advertising, no user accounts.
• Installation asks for access to no site at all. Chrome asks for the
  permission only when you enter your own server address, and only for that
  address.
• There are no content scripts: the extension does not touch the pages you
  browse.
• Credentials are stored in the extension's own storage on this machine.

The interface is in English, with Finnish available in the settings.

--- THE PRIVACY PRACTICES TAB ----------------------------------------------
The fields below are written for the reviewer. The character limits are 1,000
per field; all of them fit.


SINGLE PURPOSE DESCRIPTION (815 characters)
-------------------------------------------
Kepuli-TV does one thing: it plays back the user's own IPTV subscription in the browser.

The user enters the address and credentials of an Xtream Codes server they already pay for. The extension lists that account's live channels, movies and series and plays them in its own tab, together with the programme guide the server provides.

The extension ships no content, no channel list and no account of its own; it is useless without an account the user brings. It has no content scripts and never touches the pages the user browses - clicking the toolbar icon opens the extension's own tab and nothing else. Every feature serves that single purpose: fetching the account's catalogue, remuxing MKV to fMP4 so Chrome can play it, decoding AC-3/E-AC-3/DTS audio, showing subtitles, and remembering playback positions.


STORAGE JUSTIFICATION (855 characters)
--------------------------------------
chrome.storage.local holds only what the player cannot work without:

1) The connection the user configures - their own IPTV server address, port, username and password - so the credentials are not retyped every time the player tab is opened.
2) Playback preferences: interface language, subtitle language and size, and stream mode.
3) The user's own lists: favourites, recently watched, and the resume position per movie and episode.

All of it is written by the user's own actions inside the extension's own tab, stays on the device, and is never transmitted anywhere - the extension has no backend and no analytics. Without this permission the user would have to re-enter their server address and credentials on every launch, and no favourite, history entry or resume position could survive a page reload.


UNLIMITEDSTORAGE JUSTIFICATION (688 characters)
-----------------------------------------------
The catalogue returned by the user's own IPTV server is cached in IndexedDB so it is not re-downloaded on every launch. These catalogues are large: tens of megabytes of JSON for channels, movies and series, and programme guide data per channel. That routinely exceeds the default quota Chrome grants an extension. When the quota is hit, cache writes fail and the user waits through a full re-download of a multi-megabyte listing on every navigation, which makes the extension unusable on normal IPTV accounts.

No personal data is stored under this permission - only a copy of the listing the user's own server returned. The cache can be cleared from the extension's settings at any time.


HOST PERMISSION JUSTIFICATION (686 characters)
----------------------------------------------
This field appears only if the store asks about host permissions. The manifest
declares optional_host_permissions rather than host_permissions, so the field
may not appear at all.

The extension does not request host access at install time. http://*/* and https://*/* are declared under optional_host_permissions only, and Chrome asks for the permission at runtime - for the single origin the user typed - the first time they save their own server address.

The permission is needed because an IPTV server can be at any address and that address is unknowable before installation; the user brings it. Access is used solely for fetch/XHR against that server: the player_api.php endpoint, and the HLS/TS segment requests made by hls.js and mpegts.js. The extension has no content scripts, injects nothing into web pages, and reads no data from any site the user browses.


ARE YOU USING REMOTE CODE?
--------------------------
The choice: "No, I am not using Remote code".

Justification (706 characters), if the field is open:

No remote code is used. Every script and binary the extension executes ships inside the package: mpegts.js and hls.js under vendor/, and an audio decoder compiled from FFmpeg to WebAssembly at vendor/ffaudio/, loaded with fetch() from the extension's own chrome-extension:// path. There are no <script> tags referencing external files, no remote module imports, no eval() and no new Function().

The manifest CSP is "script-src 'self' 'wasm-unsafe-eval'; object-src 'self'". The 'wasm-unsafe-eval' keyword is required only to instantiate that packaged .wasm file; no remote origin appears in script-src. Network requests fetch media and catalogue data from the IPTV server the user configures - never code.


DATA USAGE — THE CHECKBOXES
---------------------------
Tick:
  [x] Authentication information

Leave every other box unticked:
  [ ] Personally identifiable information — no name, e-mail address or
      identity number is asked for
  [ ] Health information
  [ ] Financial and payment information — the extension handles no payments
  [ ] Personal communications
  [ ] Location — no geolocation; the user's own server sees their IP, but that
      is the provider's traffic and not something the extension collects
  [ ] Web history — no content scripts, no access to browsing history
  [ ] User activity — favourites, history and resume positions stay on the
      device and are sent nowhere
  [ ] Website content — the extension does not read the contents of the pages
      being browsed

Why Authentication information is ticked even though nothing is sent to the
developer: the user enters their IPTV credentials into the extension, and in
the manner of the Xtream Codes API those are passed in the request URL to the
user's own server. The store's definition of "user data" covers data received
from the user, and asking for credentials without the tick is a known
rejection reason ("incomplete data disclosure"). The tick is not a problem: all
three certifications (not sold, not used outside the single purpose, not used
for creditworthiness) hold true, and the privacy policy states that the
credentials stay on the device and travel only to the user's own server.

Certifications: tick all three.
